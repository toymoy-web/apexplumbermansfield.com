What To Do When A Pipe Bursts In Your Mansfield Home.

The first five minutes after a pipe bursts matter more than almost anything that happens after, here is exactly what to do, in order.

Step One: Shut Off The Main Water Supply.

This is the single most important thing you can do, and it matters more than calling anyone, including us, first. The main shutoff valve is usually located where the water line enters the house, often near the water meter or in a utility area. Turning it off stops the flow immediately, which limits water damage more than anything else that happens in the next hour.

If you do not already know where your main shutoff valve is, finding it now, before an emergency, is worth five minutes of your time.

Step Two: Address Electrical Safety.

If water is anywhere near an outlet, switch, or electrical panel, do not enter that area. If it is safe to reach the breaker without stepping into standing water, shutting off power to the affected area is worth doing. If reaching the breaker means walking through water, wait for a professional rather than risking it.

Step Three: Limit The Damage, Then Document It.

Once the water is off, move furniture, rugs, and valuables away from the affected area if it is safe to do so. If there is time before cleanup begins, photograph the damage, this matters for insurance and is far easier to do before things are moved or dried.

Why Pipes Burst In Mansfield.

Texas homes generally are not built for hard freezes the way northern climates are, and pipes running through exterior walls, attics, or garages often have limited insulation as a result. This became a statewide problem during Winter Storm Uri in February 2021, when a prolonged hard freeze caused widespread residential pipe bursts across Texas, including North Texas. It also led the state legislature to pass freeze-protection requirements for infrastructure afterward. Any repair or repiping work still has to meet the same International Plumbing Code (IPC) requirements Texas enforces statewide.

Outside of a major freeze event, old, corroded, or already-weakened pipe failing under normal water pressure is the other common cause we see, independent of the weather.

After The Water Is Off.

The break itself gets repaired first to stop the immediate problem. If the pipe failed due to a freeze or general age rather than an isolated accident, it is worth a closer look. Flag whether nearby sections of the same run are at similar risk. A pipe that failed once under those conditions can fail again elsewhere along the same line. See our burst pipe repair page for how that diagnosis and repair works.

If the same pipe has now failed more than once, or the home still has original galvanized supply lines from an earlier construction era, it may be worth a broader conversation about repiping rather than repairing the same aging system one break at a time.

Where water damage has already occurred to flooring, drywall, or framing, that cleanup and drying process is a separate service from the plumbing repair itself, see our water damage restoration page for what that involves.

Preventing The Next One.

Before the next hard freeze, a few basic steps meaningfully reduce the risk of a repeat burst pipe. Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, insulate exposed pipes in a garage or crawlspace, and let faucets served by exposed pipes drip slightly during a hard freeze. Moving water is far less likely to freeze solid than water sitting still in the line.

Knowing where your main shutoff valve is matters. Confirm that it still turns freely rather than being seized from years of not being touched. It is worth checking before you need it in an emergency rather than during one. A valve that will not fully close is a real problem, and it is one we can replace before it matters rather than after.

When One Burst Pipe Points To A Bigger Issue.

A single burst pipe from an isolated freeze event is usually just that, an isolated event. A pattern of repeated pipe failures, especially in a home with original galvanized supply lines from an earlier construction era, points to something more systemic. The pipe material itself is reaching the end of its service life, rather than one unlucky section failing.

If your home has had more than one pipe failure in recent years, it is worth a broader conversation about the bigger picture. The same goes if you know the supply lines are original galvanized steel. Repairing individual breaks one at a time versus a full repiping that addresses the underlying cause once rather than the same symptom repeatedly is worth weighing honestly.

Common Questions.

Where is my main water shutoff valve likely to be in a Mansfield home?

Most commonly near the water meter or in a utility area where the main line enters the house, it is worth locating and testing it before an emergency happens.

Can a pipe burst days after a freeze has already ended?

Yes, a pipe that survived a freeze without visibly failing can have internal damage that shows up as a leak or burst once it thaws and normal pressure returns.

Is calling a plumber or my insurance company first the right move?

Shutting off the water comes first, after that either call can come next, we can document the damage and repair for your insurance records either way.

How can I prevent frozen pipes before the next hard freeze?

Insulate exposed pipes, disconnect outdoor hoses, and let faucets served by exposed lines drip slightly during a hard freeze, moving water resists freezing far better than still water.

Should I be worried about pipes in my attic during a Texas winter?

Yes, attic-run pipes are often under-insulated in Texas construction since hard freezes are less common here than up north, they are worth checking or insulating before a forecasted cold snap.
