Hydro Jetting Vs Snaking: Which Drain Fix Do You Need?.

Both clear a clogged drain, but they solve genuinely different problems, and picking the wrong one means paying for the same fix twice.

What Each One Actually Does.

A drain snake, sometimes called an auger, is a flexible cable that physically breaks through or hooks onto whatever is blocking a pipe, clearing a path for water to flow again. It solves the immediate blockage but does not clean the rest of the pipe's interior surface.

Hydro jetting uses a hose that sprays high-pressure water in all directions as it moves through the pipe, scouring the entire inside diameter clean rather than just punching a hole through the clog. It removes grease coating, mineral scale, and root intrusion along the whole length treated, not just at the blockage point.

When Snaking Is The Right Call.

For a single, isolated clog, hair in a bathroom drain, a foreign object stuck in a toilet trap, a one-time kitchen sink backup, snaking is fast, effective, and does not require the setup jetting does. If a drain has never given you trouble before and this looks like a one-off, snaking is usually the right first step. See our drain cleaning and unclogging page for how that standard service works.

When Jetting Is Actually The Better Fix.

  • The same drain has needed snaking more than once in the past year.
  • A camera inspection shows grease buildup coating the interior of a kitchen line.
  • Root intrusion has been confirmed in an older sewer lateral.
  • The property is a restaurant or commercial kitchen dealing with recurring grease buildup.
  • A property manager is fielding repeated complaints about the same line across a multi-unit building.

Why Mansfield Sees More Of The Jetting-Level Problems.

North Texas' clay-heavy soil shifts with the seasons, and older sewer laterals in this area are genuinely prone to root intrusion as tree roots seek out any moisture and small gaps at aging pipe joints. Grease buildup in kitchen lines is the other frequent cause we see, independent of a home's age or construction era. It accumulates gradually along the entire pipe wall rather than at one single point, which is exactly why a spot treatment like snaking keeps needing to be repeated on the same line rather than actually resolving it.

How We Decide Which One To Use.

Rather than guessing, we typically run a camera down the line first when a clog is recurring or the cause is not obvious. That footage shows exactly what is actually going on, a one-time blockage, grease buildup along the pipe wall, root intrusion at a joint, or in some cases a structural problem like a cracked or offset pipe that neither snaking nor jetting alone will actually fix. See our hydro jetting page for what that diagnosis and jetting process looks like, or our sewer line repair page if the camera inspection points to actual pipe damage rather than buildup.

A Note On Pressure And Older Pipe.

Jetting pressure that is safe for a sound, modern pipe can be too aggressive for an older, already-compromised line, which is another reason the camera inspection matters before jetting rather than after. Pressure is calibrated to the pipe's actual material and condition on every job, not applied at a single default setting regardless of what is being cleaned.

What A Jetting Visit Actually Looks Like.

The line is typically accessed through an existing cleanout rather than requiring any new opening to be cut. A camera pass confirms the pipe's condition first, then the jetting hose is fed through and run at a pressure and nozzle setting matched to what that camera footage showed. A second camera pass afterward confirms the line is actually clear, not just that water is flowing again for the moment.

For a residential line, this whole process typically takes under an hour. A commercial kitchen line with heavier buildup, or a longer sewer lateral with confirmed root intrusion at multiple points, can take longer depending on the length and condition of what needs to be cleared.

Property Managers And Recurring Complaints.

A genuine property-management thread shows up regularly in our commercial calls, work orders placed on behalf of tenants dealing with a slow or backed up drain in a shared or aging line. Where the same complaint keeps recurring across a multi-unit property, jetting the shared line is often the fix that actually resolves it, rather than sending a technician out to snake the same spot every few months. See our commercial plumbing page for how we coordinate that kind of ongoing service directly with a property manager.

Common Questions.

Is hydro jetting more expensive than snaking?

Generally yes, since it addresses the whole pipe rather than a single blockage, but for a recurring clog it is often the more cost-effective choice over time compared to repeated snaking visits.

Can jetting damage an older Mansfield home's pipes?

Properly calibrated jetting is safe for sound pipe, which is why we check the line's condition with a camera first on older lines before jetting rather than jetting blind.

How do I know if my drain needs jetting instead of snaking?

If the same drain has needed clearing more than once recently, or you know the line has root intrusion or heavy grease buildup, that is a strong sign jetting is the better fix.

Does hydro jetting work on older Mansfield homes with clay sewer lines?

Yes, provided the camera inspection confirms the pipe is structurally sound, jetting is calibrated carefully to the pipe's actual material and condition regardless of the line's exact age.

Can hydro jetting be done without a full camera inspection first?

It is possible, but skipping the camera step means jetting blind on an unknown pipe condition, which is why we recommend it as standard practice rather than an optional upsell.
